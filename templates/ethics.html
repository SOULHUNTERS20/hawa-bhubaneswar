Ethics & consent

Your data, in plain language

What we collect, what we never collect, and how the data is used.

✓ What we collect

  • A random participant ID (like BBSR-Z3-482) and your consent
  • City and residential zone (one of 5 large areas, not your address)
  • Age group, gender and occupation / workplace type (SOP steps 4–6)
  • Your daily time-activity diary: time blocks, type of place and activity (SOP step 7)
  • Survey answers on how you act on air-quality information (SOP steps 8, 10)
  • Hotspot reports with a public landmark (SOP step 9)
  • Community readings: AQI / PM2.5, temperature, how the air felt, an optional note (max {{ note_max }} characters)

✗ What we never collect

  • Names, emails or phone numbers
  • Exact addresses or GPS location
  • Photos, contacts or device information
  • Health or medical information
  • Tracking cookies or advertising IDs

Consent and who can take part

Taking part is voluntary and needs your consent at registration. Participants must be 18 or older. You can stop at any time. The study follows the {{ cfg.GROUP_NAME }} Standard Operating Protocol: {{ cfg.REGISTRATION_TARGET }} participants, {{ cfg.STUDY_DAYS }} days of diaries, a survey before and after the alerts, and a hotspot register.

How your ID works

When you register, the server makes a random ID and saves it with your zone, profile answers and the date. Your browser remembers the ID (in "localStorage") so you don't have to type it again. Nobody, including the project team, can tell who is behind an ID unless you tell them.

Who can see the data

  • Public: community readings (Data page, downloadable), the hotspot register (without who reported it), and group totals for profiles, diaries and surveys.
  • Project team only: individual profiles, diaries and survey answers. With only {{ cfg.REGISTRATION_TARGET }} participants, combining age, gender, occupation and zone could identify someone, so these are never published row by row.

Please don't write names, addresses or anything personal in notes or hotspot reports.

What the data is for

  • Academic research and student projects about urban air quality
  • Citizen science: showing how air differs between neighbourhoods
  • Comparing what people experience with model and official estimates
  • Estimating personal exposure (PAQE) and whether alerts change behaviour (DRS)

Hotspot reports record what was seen and where. They are verified by students and never treated as proof of who caused the pollution.

It is not official government data and must not be used for medical decisions. Low-cost monitors and phone apps can be inaccurate.

Quality checks

Readings that are very different from the live model value for the same zone are flagged, not deleted. Sometimes the community is right and the model is wrong, which is exactly what we want to find out. The project team may remove obvious spam or test entries.

Ask Hawa (AI assistant)

Ask Hawa uses Google’s Gemini AI on its free tier. When you ask a question, your question and a summary of this site’s air data are sent to Google. On the free tier, Google may use them to improve its products, and people at Google may read them. So:

  • Never type personal details (names, phone numbers, addresses, health records) into Ask Hawa.
  • Ask Hawa is for people aged 18 or older, as Google’s terms require.
  • We don’t save your questions on this site, and Ask Hawa never sees participant IDs.
  • AI can be wrong. Its answers are not official and not medical advice.

Your choices

  • Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time.
  • You can remove your ID from your browser on the My ID page.
  • To have your readings deleted, contact the project team with your participant ID.
